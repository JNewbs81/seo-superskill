# Editorial SEO and Resource Guides

Use editorial content to answer real customer questions that a service or location page does not fully answer. A blog is optional; a maintained resource library can serve the same purpose.

## Select useful topics

Use owner knowledge, customer questions, Search Console queries when available, and gaps in existing pages. Distinguish observed demand from topic hypotheses. Choose one primary intent per article and map it to an existing service/location page before writing. Consolidate overlapping pages rather than creating articles that compete for the same intent.

Useful patterns include preparation checklists, explanations of a verified process, comparisons that resolve confusion, and local resources with maintained official links. Do not generate posts simply to meet a publishing frequency or multiply city-keyword variants.

## Evidence and identity

Give each brief a reader question, concise answer, unique value, factual sources, intended internal links, reviewer needs, and a review trigger. Use current primary sources for changing facts. For legal, financial, or medical topics, distinguish general process information from professional advice and do not invent business policies or eligibility rules.

Attribute only real authorship and review. Do not invent expert bylines, approvals, credentials, client stories, citations or publication dates. A business can be the organizational author when appropriate. Show dates and authorship visibly if they are in schema. Do not change dates just to appear fresh.

## Build and connect

Use stable crawlable URLs, a resource hub, breadcrumbs, unique metadata, answer-first headings, accessible content and relevant internal links. Link from a service/location page when the guide answers a question that naturally arises there; link back to the appropriate service action. Avoid unrelated sitewide keyword links.

Add Article or BlogPosting JSON-LD only when its properties match visible content. BreadcrumbList must match navigation. Keep author, publisher, headline, canonical URL and dates accurate. FAQ markup is optional and does not guarantee a Google rich result. Do not add fake ratings or claim guaranteed AI citations.

## Quality and upkeep

Check source accuracy, originality, scope, clarity, internal links, accessible mobile rendering and live indexability before publication. Run the static checker on full output; also verify HTTP responses and robots headers. Put only complete canonical indexable pages in the sitemap.

Prioritize updates based on changed facts, broken resources, customer feedback, and Search Console evidence. Evaluate impressions/clicks and useful query coverage over time; separate a lack of data from poor performance. Consolidate or redirect redundant content deliberately. Paid backlinks, mass posting, keyword density targets and fabricated local expertise are not substitutes for useful material.

## Report

State what was published, which customer question it answers, supporting evidence, links to the relevant service/location pages, verification completed and any factual or measurement gaps. Keep proposed topics separate from deployed pages and accepted indexing requests separate from actual indexing.
